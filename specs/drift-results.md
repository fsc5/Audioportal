# Task 12 drift handoff

## Tuning and measurement

- Receiver target: configured 10–100 ms jitter prefill, with two 5 ms blocks of downstream queue headroom. Jitter release feedback targets configured prefill minus half a 5 ms slot because occupancy oscillates as slots are emitted. Device padding and resampler filter delay are measured in the total but excluded from the adjustable portion of the target; they remain added latency.
- Both controllers filter occupancy over 2 seconds, with proportional gain 200 ppm per 5 ms (240 frames), correction limited to ±1000 ppm and a request slew limit of 2000 ppm/s. Playback's existing resampler ramps the applied request by at most 100 ppm per 5 ms. Units: input frames consumed per nominal output frame; above 1 drains faster. Native 48 kHz-to-device-rate conversion remains independent.
- Occupancy combines jitter stored blocks, the four-block playback queue, pending partial input, converted output, filter delay, and WASAPI padding, converting output frames to 48 kHz input frames. The atomics are sampled independently; padding is last-refill estimation, not a presentation timestamp. The 10-second receiver stderr report includes jitter blocks, total milliseconds, both requested ratios, cumulative substituted input frames, network losses, local scheduling skips, and playback-queue evictions. The latter two are accumulated across peer timeout; loss and missing-input are never reset before a new Start.
- A timeout drops stale queued audio, resets both controllers and the virtual playout timeline, and returns to listening. Existing jitter catch-up skips overdue slots in bounded work, and both storage queues evict stale audio; the controller limits speed change rather than integrating prolonged backlog.

## Verification and hardware follow-up

- Deterministic tests model separate uninterrupted hours at +200 ppm and -200 ppm, plus an hour with +200 ppm reversed to -200 ppm after 30 minutes; all use a separate -100 ppm playback clock. Packet delivery jitter repeats 0/2/7/1 ms; one packet per 997 is dropped. They advance simulated time in 5 ms ticks, exercising receiver packet acceptance, jitter release, bounded queue and both feedback paths, and check occupancy relative to the configured target, both ratio directions, visible loss and queue evictions, missing playback input, and actual timeout/reset/new-timeline behavior. PCM conversion and WASAPI callbacks are not simulated.

On two Windows PCs, follow `session-results.md`'s first-stream setup, run the receiver for at least one hour with continuous routed application audio, and preserve the receiver's stderr log (one line every 10 seconds). Repeat with roles swapped. A phone/loopback recording or synchronized reference signal is needed to measure actual end-to-end latency; estimated occupancy alone is insufficient.
